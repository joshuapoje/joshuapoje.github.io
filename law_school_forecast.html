What drives the cliff

U.S. birth rates fell sharply after the 2008 financial crisis. That cohort reaches traditional college age in 2026–2028, creating a structural — not cyclical — contraction in the higher education pipeline. Nathan Grawe's research projects a 15% national undergraduate enrollment decline, with Midwest and Northeast regions facing 25–30% drops.

The law school lag

Law school is downstream of the bachelor's pipeline by 3–4 years. Undergraduate pressure arriving in 2026–2030 reaches law schools in 2029–2034. The LSAT applicant pool is a leading indicator — stress there typically precedes enrollment declines by 12–18 months.

The amplification problem

Law school applicant pools tend to over-react to demographic signals. When the college-going population shrinks, the marginal applicant who might have enrolled at a lower-ranked school often doesn't apply at all. This is why the amplification factor matters — the raw demographic signal is not 1:1 with law school enrollment impact.

What this means for the profession

Fewer JD graduates narrows the pipeline into the bar — and into bar association membership. The contraction hits public interest, rural, and government pipelines hardest. The diversity pipeline is most at risk, as first-generation and underrepresented students skew toward institutions in the highest-distress tiers.
